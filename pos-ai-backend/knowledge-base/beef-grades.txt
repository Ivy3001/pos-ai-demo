Title: What's Your Beef: Prime, Choice or Select?
Source: U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA)
URL: https://www.usda.gov/about-usda/news/blog/whats-your-beef-prime-choice-or-select
Checked: November 2026

USDA beef grades are assigned by USDA meat graders using official standards from the Agricultural Marketing Service. Beef is graded for quality (tenderness, juiciness and flavor) and for yield (the amount of usable lean meat).

Prime beef comes from young, well-fed cattle and has the most marbling. It is mostly sold to restaurants and hotels. Prime steaks and roasts are best cooked with dry heat, such as grilling, broiling or roasting.

Choice beef is high quality with less marbling than Prime. Choice rib and loin cuts, such as ribeye steaks and rib roasts, are very tender, juicy and flavorful. Less tender Choice cuts can be cooked with dry heat if not overcooked, and are most tender when braised, roasted or simmered with a little liquid in a covered pan.

Select beef is leaner, with less marbling than Choice. It is fairly tender but can be less juicy and flavorful. Only the tender Select cuts should be cooked with dry heat; other Select cuts are best marinated before cooking or braised.

Standard and Commercial grades are often sold ungraded or as store-brand meat. Utility, Cutter and Canner grades are rarely sold at retail and are mainly used for ground beef and processed products.
