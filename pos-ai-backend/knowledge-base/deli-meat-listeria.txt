Title: Listeria and Deli Meats and Cheeses
Source: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)
URL: https://www.cdc.gov/listeria/outbreaks/deli-11-22/
Checked: November 2026

Listeria is a germ that can contaminate deli meats and deli-sliced cheeses. It spreads easily in deli areas and can survive for a long time in deli display cases and on equipment such as slicers.

People at higher risk of severe Listeria illness are pregnant women, adults aged 65 or older, and people with a weakened immune system from certain medical conditions or treatments.

People at higher risk, including pregnant customers, should heat deli meat and deli-sliced cheese to an internal temperature of 165°F, or until steaming hot, before eating. It is fine to let the food cool after heating.

For higher-risk people, cheeses that do not need reheating include hard cheeses that are not sliced at the deli counter (such as cheddar and parmesan), feta, string cheese, cottage cheese and cream cheese.
