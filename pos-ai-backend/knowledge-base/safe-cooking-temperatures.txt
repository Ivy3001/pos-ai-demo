Title: Cook to a Safe Minimum Internal Temperature
Source: FoodSafety.gov (USDA, FDA and CDC)
URL: https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures
Checked: November 2026

Whole cuts of beef, veal, lamb, bison and goat, such as steaks, roasts and chops, should be cooked to an internal temperature of at least 145°F (63°C), then allowed to rest for 3 minutes before carving or eating.

Whole cuts of pork, such as pork chops, pork steaks and pork roasts, should be cooked to at least 145°F (63°C), then allowed to rest for 3 minutes.

Ground meat and sausage made from beef or pork, such as ground beef, burgers and bratwurst, should be cooked to at least 160°F (71°C). Ground meat needs a higher temperature than whole steaks because grinding mixes surface bacteria throughout the meat.

Raw (fresh, uncooked) ham should be cooked to at least 145°F (63°C), then rested for 3 minutes.

Precooked ham that is being reheated should reach 165°F (74°C). A precooked ham that was packaged in a USDA-inspected plant can be reheated to 140°F (60°C).

All poultry, including chicken and turkey (whole birds, cuts, ground poultry, giblets, poultry sausage and stuffing), should be cooked to at least 165°F (74°C).

Leftovers of any type, and casseroles with or without meat, should be reheated to 165°F (74°C).

The only reliable way to know meat is cooked safely is to check the internal temperature with a food thermometer placed in the thickest part of the meat. Color alone is not a reliable sign of doneness.
